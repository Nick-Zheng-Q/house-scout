# Purchase checks

For the relevant jurisdiction and native terms, read [market guidance](international-markets.en.md); for period conversion, cash, transport passes and financing assumptions read [cost calculations](cost-comparison.en.md). Mainland China platform steps remain in [platforms](platforms.en.md).

For new construction, distinguish a show unit from the actual unit, presale versus completed home, contracted finish and completion date, and remedies for delay. Do not mix gross/internal/advertised “bonus” area. For resale, check specific unit, owner/co-owner, registered use, encumbrances or restrictions, occupant/lease, condition and arrears. Listings do not prove these; use appropriate local official records, written contracts and professional inspection.

Eligibility to buy, minimum down payment, loan amount/rate, taxes, school/registration rights and subsidies vary by place, property and buyer. When decisive, consult current official rules and buyer-specific lender documents. Separate “may apply”, “preliminary discussion”, and “formally approved”. An ad does not establish financing, a tax rate or a negotiable price.

Itemize asking versus agreed price, proposed own funds and financing assumptions, taxes, broker/transaction fees, repairs/renovation, recurring maintenance/property charges and mortgage payment. Show assumptions and sensitivity. Without an agreed price and approved financing, cash-to-close and monthly payment are **scenarios**, not confirmed within budget. Identify earnest/holding/deposit payments, payee, timing and refund conditions under the actual contract and local rules. Do not double-count an amount credited toward the purchase price; a generic script must not invent tax or lending eligibility.

At the viewing check daylight, noise, damp, cracks, leaks, services and shared spaces; recommend specialists for structural, environmental or other technical risks. Before signing, verify title/authority, delivery inventory, defects and arrears, financing-failure terms, and breach/exit clauses. Missing decisive evidence means “obtain evidence first” or “not recommended”, not a safe purchase.
